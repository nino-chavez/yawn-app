# Blind comparison — family rethink concepts

Scope note: I opened only the 21 frames named in the brief. Names below follow the corner labels: Concept 1 Notebook, Concept 2 Rail, Concept 3 Record.

## State 1 — Mac, after

Concept 1 Notebook. The eye lands first on the bold title "Launch checklist review" with the eight typed lines beneath it, second on the blue selected row in the sidebar, third on the player bar and the transcript below, where one row (11:26) has a pale blue highlight. It answers what is happening (a finished meeting, "Transcript ready"), who (You and Them labels per turn), when (date, time, 24:18 in the small header line) and what I can do (play, find in transcript, Record at top right). What is next is only implied: nothing says "check a line against the transcript", but the highlighted 11:26 row and the 11:30 playhead suggest it.

Concept 2 Rail. The eye lands first on the typed lines in the center column, each with a dot on a vertical line and a timestamp at left (0:48, 2:10, 4:05, and so on). Second it goes to the shaded transcript excerpt at the right for 11:26, third to the small player row at the top. It answers when very well, since every note has a time. Who is only in the 8pt "You" and "Them" tags. What I can do is thin: a "Full transcript" link at top right is the only route to the whole transcript, so checking a detail needs an extra step. A caption at lower left reads "Depends on a moment per typed line (not built)", which is a build disclosure that sits on the frame.

Concept 3 Record. The eye lands first on the scrubber with tick marks across the full width, second on the boxed "YOUR NOTES" block, third on the dense transcript table. It answers everything, but the notes are demoted to a gray two-column box with small monospace text, while the transcript rows are larger than the notes. That inverts the stated priority (the person's notes are the point).

Best for this state: Concept 1. Deciding reason: the typed notes sit at the top as the largest body text on the page (about 15px, left-aligned under a 24px title, filling roughly the top third), and the transcript is a secondary section below a divider. In Concept 3 the notes live in a 8-line-high gray box whose text is smaller than the transcript rows. In Concept 2 the notes are large but the transcript is clipped to one excerpt per note.

Web-page feel: Concept 3 reads most like a web dashboard (the monospace uppercase section labels "YOUR NOTES" and "TRANSCRIPT", the bordered notes card, the ruled table rows). Concept 1's pale blue transcript highlight and the "Find in transcript" pill are fine. Concept 2's "Full transcript >" text link is slightly web-like.

Tired misreads: in Concept 1 the player shows 11:30 while the highlighted transcript row is 11:26, which looks like a mismatch. In Concept 3 the playhead reads 11:26 with the same row highlighted, which is cleaner, but the ticks above the scrubber have no legend, so a tired person will not know what they mean. In Concept 2 "+ 18 turns" separators between excerpts look like clickable controls and could be misread as hidden content that was lost.

Dark appearance: all three hold up. Concept 1 dark is the clearest, with white text on near-black and a bright blue selection. Concept 2 dark loses the highlighted excerpt contrast slightly (gray box on dark gray) and its dim "+ N turns" lines are low contrast. Concept 3 dark is legible but its monospace labels are very dim.

## State 2 — Mac, during

Concept 1 Notebook. The eye lands first on the red "Recording 4:12" pill at top right, second on the blinking caret at the end of the third typed line, third on the collapsed "Live transcript · 9 turns" at the bottom left. What is happening: clear. When: elapsed 4:12 and "Started 10:05 AM". What I can do: pause and stop buttons sit next to the pill. Who: not shown. The page is almost entirely empty white, which is correct for someone typing in a call, and the sidebar row says "Recording now".

Concept 2 Rail. The eye lands first on the red-dotted third line at 4:05 in red, second on the red pill, third on the faint dots above. Each typed line carries a time stamp as it is written, and the current one is in red. This is the best at showing "my notes are being tied to the audio as I type". The bottom caption "Depends on a moment per typed line (not built)" tells a viewer this is not real yet. There is no transcript access at all during recording.

Concept 3 Record. The eye lands first on the scrubber with a red diamond at the right end, second on the notes box, third on the live transcript table with "Listening ..." at the bottom. This shows the most (a live transcript of the call, who is speaking, the current moment). For someone in a meeting this is a lot to look at while typing, and the transcript competes with the notes for attention.

Best for this state: Concept 1. Deciding reason: during a call the writing surface should be the only thing in the viewport, and in Concept 1 the typing area fills the whole pane below the 24px title with the transcript reduced to a single 12px line at the bottom edge. Concept 3 puts nine transcript rows (about 60 percent of the pane height) beside the notes.

Web-page feel: Concept 3 again (bordered card, monospace labels). The Concept 2 timestamp column is native-feeling.

Tired misreads: in Concept 2 the "4:05" in red on the last line could be read as the current recording time, when the pill says 4:12. In Concept 3 the scrubber with no right-hand end time (a dash) looks broken. In Concept 1 the sidebar title "Recording now" and the window title "Meeting · Oct 15, 2026" give two names for the same thing.

## State 3 — Mac, interrupted

Concept 1 Notebook. The eye lands first on the tan banner "This recording was interrupted.", second on the blue "Transcribe the audio" button, third on the single typed line below. All five questions are answered inside the banner: what happened, when (3:36 PM), what is kept (first 6:41 of audio, on this Mac), what is missing (no transcript), what I can do (Transcribe, Move to Trash). The orange status dot in the sidebar row matches the banner.

Concept 2 Rail. Same banner, with a dark primary button, and below it the typed line at 1:35 on the rail ending in an amber square labelled "Recording stopped here" at 6:41. That marker shows where the audio ends relative to the note, which is the one thing the other two do not show visually in the notes area. The dark button is more muted than a native blue default button.

Concept 3 Record. A scrubber with a diagonal-less amber end mark labelled "Stopped 3:36 PM", the banner, then a Notes box and a "TRANSCRIPT: No transcript was made." block. It states the absence twice (banner and empty section), which is a little heavy but unambiguous.

Best for this state: Concept 2. Deciding reason: the amber square and the words "Recording stopped here" at 6:41 sit directly under the person's own note, so they can see in place that their note was written at 1:35 and the audio ends at 6:41. Concept 1 has the same banner but no in-page marker; Concept 3 puts the stop marker on a separate scrubber above.

Web-page feel: the banner in all three is a flat rounded card with a left or full tint, which is closer to a web alert than a Mac sheet or inline notice. Concept 2's left amber bar makes it the most web-like.

Tired misreads: "Move to Trash" sits beside the primary action with equal size and similar weight in Concepts 1 and 3, so a tired click could trash audio the person wanted to transcribe. Concept 2 reduces this slightly by making the primary dark and the secondary white. The text "first 6:41 of audio" and the title time "3:30 PM · 6:41 captured" use the same number two ways, which could be read as a clock time.

## State 4 — Mac, Settings

Concept 1 Notebook. A small window with a native-looking toolbar of five tabs (Recording, Transcription, Speakers, Storage, Updates) with placeholder gray squares for icons and one blue square for the active tab. The content shown is only the Recording tab: Microphone and System audio, both "Authorized", a "Check again" button and a reassurance that nothing leaves the Mac. This is the most native of the three (it matches a macOS Settings window).

Concept 2 Rail. A window with a left sidebar of five items and the same Recording content in the right pane. The sidebar style matches the newer macOS System Settings. Content is identical to Concept 1.

Concept 3 Record. One long scrolling window with all five sections stacked under uppercase monospace headings, a toggle, status words in monospace capitals (AUTHORIZED, SELECTED, READY), and the privacy line at the bottom. It is the most informative in one view (it shows 1.8 GB, 7 meetings, version 0.6.16, a 1.1 GB model download), but it reads as a web settings page.

Best for this state: Concept 1. Deciding reason: its five-tab toolbar at the top of a compact window (about 575px wide) is the standard Mac Settings layout and shows the permission status the person most needs first. Concept 3's single scroll shows the most but has no navigation at all and is the only one with monospace capitals in status values.

Web-page feel: Concept 3 (the single long page, the uppercase monospace section labels, the toggle with a dark track). In Concepts 1 and 2 the placeholder icon squares are a comp artifact, not a design fault.

Tired misreads: in Concept 3 "SELECTED" next to Apple speech and a "Download" button next to Yawn Small could be read as two competing choices. In Concepts 1 and 2 only one tab is visible, so the person cannot tell what the others hold.

## State 6 — iPad, during

All three frames are nearly identical in layout: handwriting on a page, a left time rail with dots and timestamps (0:18 through 4:12), a centered red "Recording 4:12" pill, a red Stop button at top right, and a pen, eraser and undo column at right.

Concept 1 Notebook. The eye lands first on the large handwriting, second on the red pill, third on the red Stop button. The pen tool is blue (active), which says which tool is in hand. Each line has a timestamp, so the rail answers when. Who is not shown, which is right for one person in a room.

Concept 2 Rail. Same, on a warm off-white page. The pen tool is dark rather than blue, so the active tool is not marked in color. The current time 4:12 and the last line are in red.

Concept 3 Record. Same, on a cool white page. The timestamps are slightly monospaced and the Stop button is a deeper red.

Best for this state: Concept 1, by a small margin. Deciding reason: the pen icon in the right toolbar (about 20px, top of the column) is blue only in Concept 1, so the person can see the active tool; in Concepts 2 and 3 all three tool icons are the same dark gray.

Web-page feel: none strongly. The floating pill and the large red Stop in the top bar read as app chrome, not a page. The faint timestamp text at 10px on the rail is small for a person holding a pen at arm's length.

Tired misreads: the red Stop button is the largest control on the screen and sits where a thumb rests while writing, so a stray touch could end the recording. The frame gives no sign of a confirm step. The status bar says Wed Oct 14 while the Mac frames say Oct 15; this is fixture data, not a design problem.

## State 7 — iPad, after

Again nearly the same structure: handwriting on the left with a rail, a transcript column on the right, a "11:30 / 23:05" play control at the top left, title "Tue, Oct 14 · 10:02 AM · 23 min · Transcript ready" at center, five tool icons at right. The tapped line "budget $1,200 max" is boxed and its matching transcript turn (11:18) is highlighted. Handwriting after 11:30 is grayed, which tells the person where the replay is.

Concept 1 Notebook. The eye lands first on the boxed handwriting, second on the blue highlighted transcript turn on the right, third on the blue 11:30 chip on the rail. Blue is used for the whole "here is the moment" idea (chip, box fill, transcript highlight), so the link between the three is obvious at a glance. The transcript column is the widest of the three (about 40 percent of the width), with the text at a readable size.

Concept 2 Rail. Same structure in warm neutral, with the moment shown as a black chip, black box outline and a gray transcript highlight. The tie between the three elements is there but is carried by shape only; the gray highlight on the transcript is weaker.

Concept 3 Record. The transcript column is narrower in its start (it begins at the 500px mark, giving the handwriting more room) and adds a timestamp column in monospace. The right column is half-empty below 21:48. The moment chip is black. The tie is the same shape-only link as Concept 2.

All three answer: what is happening (paused at 11:30), what is next (play, or tap another line), who (none labeled; the transcript turns carry no speaker), when (rail times and the header), what I can do (the icon row).

Best for this state: Concept 1. Deciding reason: the 11:30 chip on the rail, the box around "budget $1,200 max" and the 11:18 transcript row are all the same blue, so the link from handwriting to audio to transcript can be seen without reading; in Concepts 2 and 3 these are black and light gray.

Web-page feel: the column of five unlabeled icons at the top right (pencil, eraser, undo, lines, search, share) is dense and could be mistaken for a web toolbar; the "Meetings" back pill and the pill-shaped play control are native in style. "+ 20 turns" separators in the transcript are small links that read as a web accordion.

Tired misreads: the handwriting is boxed at 11:30 but the transcript row says 11:18. A person could think the transcript is off by 12 seconds. The play control says "11:30 / 23:05" while the title says "23 min", which is consistent but two different forms. The grayed-out lines below the box could be read as erased or disabled rather than "later than the replay point". The "After the meeting" divider with "call Sam Mon" below it is useful but its label is very faint.

## Family read

Concept 2 Rail most reads as one product on two devices. The same device appears on both: a thin vertical line of dots with timestamps beside each of the person's own lines, in the same position (left of the notes). On the Mac in state 1 and 2 it is the whole page structure; on the iPad it is the left rail. A person who knows one will know the other without relearning. Concept 1 shares the iPad's blue "this moment" idea but the Mac frames have no rail, so the two devices look like two apps with a shared color. Concept 3 shares mono labels and a scrubber on Mac but the iPad frames have neither, so it is the weakest as a family, despite the iPad frames being the most similar to the Mac ones in tone (cool white and black).

Note the tension: the concept that is most one-product (Rail) is not the one that is best at each single state on the Mac (Notebook), because a rail on the Mac costs transcript room.

## Ranking

Mac: 1 Notebook, 2 Rail, 3 Record. Notebook puts the person's notes first and is the most native in every Mac state; Rail is the best at the interrupted state and the most distinctive but clips the transcript; Record shows the most but gives the transcript the larger type and reads as a web dashboard.

iPad: 1 Notebook, 2 Rail, 3 Record. Notebook and Rail are close; Notebook wins because its blue active-tool and blue moment link are visible without reading; Record is last because its transcript column leaves the page with more dead space and its moment link is the weakest.

## Red team

Strongest argument against Notebook, as a checkable claim: on the Mac in state 1, the typed notes and the transcript are two separate blocks with no visible connection between a line and a moment. The player shows 11:30, the highlighted transcript row says 11:26, and none of the eight note lines carries a time or a link. So the main reason the person came to this screen (checking one detail they wrote down) takes a scroll and a hunt, while on the iPad frames in the same concept every note line has a timestamp and a tap target. That gap makes Notebook a single-device winner and a two-device mismatch. Concept 2 on the Mac closes the gap, and its own frame admits the feature is not built.

A second checkable claim: the 11:30 versus 11:26 mismatch in state 1 and the 11:30 versus 11:18 mismatch in iPad state 7 are present in all three concepts' data, but only Notebook's Mac frame shows the two numbers on screen at once with no explanation.

## What the frames cannot tell you

- Whether a typed line can really be tied to a moment on the Mac (the Rail frames say it is not built). If not, Rail on the Mac is a mock of something that cannot ship.
- Whether the live transcript on the Mac helps or distracts while typing during a call. Concept 3 assumes it helps; Concept 1 hides it behind a disclosure.
- Whether handwriting at the iPad's 4:12 mark keeps up with a real pen or finger (latency, palm contact, line spacing), and whether the Stop button near the thumb is hit by accident.
- How the rail behaves when a note has many lines close together (the frames space them evenly, about 54px apart), or when the meeting runs two hours.
- Whether tapping a line plays from the right moment and whether the transcript auto-scrolls to follow the replay, and how fast.
- How the long "+ N turns" separators behave: whether they expand, and whether they are tap targets of a usable size.
- How the sidebar behaves with dozens of meetings, and how the Settings tabs look beyond the Recording tab.
- Real type size and contrast on the iPad at arm's length; the 10px rail timestamps and the faint "After the meeting" label need a device check.
- Dark mode on the iPad: no iPad dark frames were provided, so the family read in dark rests on the Mac frames only.
