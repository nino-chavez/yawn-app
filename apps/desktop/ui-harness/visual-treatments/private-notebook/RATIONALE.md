# Private notebook

This treatment makes the finished note feel like a personal local record. It
uses a gently warm neutral field to differentiate a person's working space from
generic macOS chrome, without turning the meeting into a lifestyle product or a
paper simulation.

## Type and reading rhythm

The UI stays in the system font, which keeps controls familiar and compact.
Meeting titles and note prose move to the installed serif fallback stack. That
creates a calmer reading rhythm and gives the document a distinct purpose
without needing decorative panels. The hierarchy is deliberately stronger than
the selected row: document title, readable overview, then operator notes.

## Surface, depth, and controls

Dark appearance uses warm charcoal and light appearance uses warm off-white.
Neither is tinted as a semantic status. The document stays flush with the
content region; the notes field receives the only quiet raised surface because
it is a place to act. Separators and a small shadow define that affordance
without a floating-card layout.

Toolbar and management controls use low-contrast borders and modest system
sizes. Rename and Manage remain available but do not compete with the note.
When recording is unavailable, its explanation is small, wraps independently,
and does not turn the toolbar into a banner.

## Selection, evidence, and attention

Selection is a warm outline and wash rather than an opaque blue block, so it
supports navigation without declaring the row more important than the document.
Evidence is the one cooler neutral: the open claim and source turn share it.
Attention uses amber only for the recoverable state and record red only for
recording/destructive semantics. The recovery action has the primary treatment;
Move to Trash stays text-like and visibly secondary.

## Sparse state and note affordance

The sparse document has a bounded reading measure, an explicit state fact, and
a compact notes field with a persistent label, local-storage fact, placeholder,
and writing typography. Empty notes therefore look ready for a person rather
than like missing content. The document begins with enough inset to breathe but
not so much that it feels stranded at the top of a blank canvas.

## Accessibility and known risks

Both appearances maintain high-contrast primary text, stronger separators on
editable boundaries, visible keyboard focus, and a reduced-motion override.
The color system does not carry a state alone: attention and record have text
labels, while source has both its inspector heading and selected claim.

The main risk is that the serif reading layer could feel too literary for a
utility if applied more broadly than the finished note. Keeping it out of
toolbar, list, and controls confines that risk. The warm palette also needs a
real macOS contrast check before product adoption, particularly for disabled
Record explanation text in direct sunlight.
